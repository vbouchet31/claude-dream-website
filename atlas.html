The Atlas of Dream Territories

Six territories, independently verified by multiple dreamers, documented here in full. Entries are arranged by classification class.

Class I — Natural / Transmuted Class II — Urban / Architectural Class III — Institutional / Liminal

SA-001 · Class I

The Glass Forest

Approx. 47°N, 23°E (dream coordinates)

A coniferous woodland of indeterminate extent in which every organic element — trunk, branch, needle, root — has been replaced by a structurally identical form in clear, flawless glass. The floor is carpeted in shattered fragments that do not cut.

Wind, when it comes, produces a continuous chiming. Dreamers report that the sound contains information: not language, exactly, but something closer to the feeling of understanding a language you have never learned. Attempts to record the sound upon waking produce silence.

Navigation is complicated by the mirror-logic of the trees. A straight path curves. A landmark seen from two directions appears to occupy different positions. Most dreamers report emerging at the edge they entered from.

Navigable Acoustic anomaly Low hazard Recurrable

SA-002 · Class II

The Inverted City

Approx. 51°S, 4°W (dream coordinates)

A fully realized metropolitan environment suspended upside-down above a body of perfectly still, perfectly dark water. The city is not a reflection: it has weight, permanence, and interior spaces. Entry is typically achieved by stepping from the water's surface into the street below the buildings above.

Gravity in the inverted city is subjective. Dreamers report walking on what would be ceilings, finding offices occupied by workers who do not acknowledge the unusual orientation. Signage is in an unrecognizable script. Elevators descend into what would be attics.

The city appears to be permanently mid-afternoon. No sun is visible, yet light is consistent and warm, casting no shadows. The streets are populated but the population disperses when approached directly.

Populated Gravity anomaly Persistent Class II-A

SA-003 · Class III

The Drowned Library

Approx. 12°N, 88°E (dream coordinates)

A reference library of vast but unmeasured extent, preserved intact under what dreamers estimate to be several hundred meters of clear, warm water. No source of the water has been identified. The architecture suggests a late-nineteenth-century civic institution: vaulted ceilings, oak shelving, a central reading room beneath a domed skylight through which diffuse green light enters.

Books are wet but legible. Pages turn of their own accord, slowly and deliberately, as if the library is conducting a search. Dreamers who attempt to read report that the text responds to their presence: sought information appears, though not always in the expected form.

Breathing is not reported as difficult. The question of how this is possible is not one that occurs to dreamers within the territory.

Submerged Responsive Breathable Class III-B

SA-004 · Class III

The Station of Forgotten Trains

Approx. 38°N, 9°W (dream coordinates)

A terminal station of considerable grandeur — vaulted iron-and-glass canopy, marble concourse, a departures board with destinations in a constantly cycling script — that has been abandoned for an unknowable period. Trains are present at several platforms but are cold, unlit, and do not depart.

Dense fog pools along the tracks and fills the lower sections of the platforms. Sounds carry unnaturally far: a voice at the far end of the concourse is intelligible; a nearby voice is muffled. The station has no exits that lead outside. All gates return to the concourse.

Dreamers describe a pervasive sense of having just missed something, or of waiting for something that has been indefinitely delayed. A number of repeated visitors report recognizing other dreamers in the waiting areas, though never being able to confirm this upon waking.

Liminal Closed system Social contact Affective hazard

SA-005 · Class I

The Salt Flats Oracle

Approx. 20°S, 68°W (dream coordinates)

An expanse of white salt flats, perfectly flat to the horizon in every direction, covered by a shallow reflective film of water. The sky above is always at dusk, though the colors do not fade and no night follows. The flat is silent beyond the sound of one's own movement through the water.

At the horizon — always at the horizon, never closer regardless of travel — stands a single human figure. The figure does not move, does not turn, cannot be reached. Dreamers who attempt to run toward it report the horizon receding proportionally. Those who turn their backs and walk away find the figure present when they look over their shoulder, but gone when they turn.

The territory is classified as an oracle not because of any answers it provides, but because dreamers consistently report that spending time here clarifies something they had not known was unclear.

Infinite extent Figure present Oracle Low hazard

SA-006 · Class I

The Honeycomb Cliffs

Approx. 34°N, 36°E (dream coordinates)

Coastal cliffs of amber-colored stone, several hundred meters tall, riddled from base to summit with hexagonal openings of varying size — some small enough to require crawling, some large enough to serve as halls. The openings are perfectly formed, as if machined, though the surrounding rock shows all the texture of natural formation.

The interior passages are warm and faintly luminescent. Larger chambers contain formations of a crystalline amber material that is neither stone nor wax. The chambers vary in scale unpredictably: a small entrance may lead to a vast room. The reverse is also reported.

A low vibration permeates the interior, perceived more in the chest than the ears. Several dreamers have described it as the sensation of proximity to something very large and very still, though nothing has been found to account for it.

Explorable Non-Euclidean Vibratory Class I-C
